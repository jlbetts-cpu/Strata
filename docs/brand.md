# Strata Neo: what it is, who it is for, and how it talks

Written 2026-09-23. `tasks/brand.md` holds the token tables and is partly
stale; this is the narrative and it is the one to read first.

---

## 1. The sentence

**A camera for the things that went right.**

You photograph a small win. It becomes a block, in its own colour, at its own
size. The blocks stack into a tower for the day, the days keep themselves, and
the photographs stay on your phone.

Everything else in the app is downstream of that sentence. If a feature cannot
be traced back to it, it is a different app.

---

## 2. The insight, said plainly

Most people cannot see what they did. The evidence of a day's effort is
scattered across rooms, messages, a tidy kitchen, a hard conversation, and
none of it is anywhere by the evening. So the day reads as nothing, whatever
was in it.

**This app is a device for making effort visible.** Not a planner: the plan is
about what has not happened. Not a habit tracker in the streak sense: a streak
is a scoreboard that punishes a gap. A record of what was true, built out of
objects you can see from across the room.

---

## 3. On neurodivergence, and where the line is

The owner asked whether the brand should centre on ADHD, on making invisible
progress visible. **The insight, yes. The clinical label, carefully.**

It is already true of the product rather than being a new angle: no streak to
break, no red, no failure state, a tower that never resets, effort counted
rather than outcomes, capture in one tap, and notification copy that was
written against Dodson on rejection sensitivity. That is an ADHD-shaped app
whether or not the word appears on the icon.

**Where we say it plainly**: the App Store description, the website, an
interview, a post. There we can name time blindness and invisible progress
directly, because that is where somebody is deciding whether this is for them.

**Where we do not**: the app's own surfaces, the name, the icon, the tagline.
Three reasons, in order of how much they cost:

1. **Apple's guideline 1.4.1 and the medical framing.** An app that presents
   itself as addressing a condition invites a different review. This account
   is already under extended review after a 4.1(a) rejection. Say what the app
   does, never what it treats.
2. **It caps the audience at one group** for a pain that is close to
   universal. Anyone who has ended a full day believing they did nothing is
   the same customer.
3. **The people it is for find it anyway**, and faster, when a product simply
   behaves correctly. The apps that have this audience rarely announce it.

**Never claim**: a therapeutic benefit, a symptom improvement, a diagnosis, a
clinical endorsement, or that the app was designed by or with clinicians
unless that becomes literally true.

**A line that does the work without the label:** *proof you did something.*

---

## 4. What the brand owes the design

If this is the promise, these stop being preferences and become obligations.
Anything that breaks one of them is a bug, not a taste question.

- **One tap to capture.** Nothing to set up first, nothing to name, nothing to
  categorise before the moment is gone.
- **A gap is never punished.** No broken streak, no red, no guilt copy, no
  empty state that implies failure. The tower is a record, not a scorecard.
- **Effort counts, not outcomes.** A thing attempted is a thing that happened.
- **Nothing depends on remembering to come back.** The app does not need you
  daily to keep its value.
- **Your photographs stay yours.** They are on the phone. Nothing about the
  app may read as watching, tracking or surveillance.
- **Every state is a finished state.** Empty, loading, error, mid-animation
  and at the largest text size. See the design doc, section 10.

---

## 5. Voice

Plain, warm, specific, never clinical and never cute. Short sentences. Say the
thing rather than the feeling about the thing.

- **No em dashes and no en dashes**, anywhere a person reads. Standing rule.
- **No surveillance language.** Never watch, track, monitor, detect or follow.
- **No exclamation marks, no emoji, no mascot voice.** The app is an
  instrument and instruments do not cheer.
- **No comparison to another app**, ever, and especially not in metadata. See
  the 4.1(a) file.
- **Second person, present tense.** "Photograph a win", not "users can log".

---

## 6. The name

**Strata Neo.** Strata because the app is layers of days. Neo because the
design is a 1990s Japanese future, the register of Neo Tokyo, an instrument in
a bright room. It also does the practical work of separating the listing from
an existing App Store app called Strata that captures everyday moments, which
is the likeliest cause of the copycat rejection.

Inside the app the word is just Strata, in one place only as a model plate in
Settings. See `tasks/app-store-metadata.md`.
