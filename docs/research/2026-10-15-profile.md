# Memories as your profile, and a wins score

Written 2026-10-14 for the pass dated 2026-10-15. Research and a code audit
only: no Swift was changed. Read against `~/Developer/Strata` at `92f64ba`
(Build 144).

The owner, verbatim:

> "an improved profile feeling more like a social media profile with a 'wins'
> score showcasing your wins, 1 for small, 2 for medium and 3 for large, making
> sure if they delete it goes down. I want the profile to feel like a profile
> and I want the memories to be the profile, merging and making it feel and
> look a lot more clean."

Tags: **(a)** research or data, **(b)** a shipping product or its own docs,
**(c)** my inference.

---

## 0. In six lines

1. **Memories becomes the profile.** Your head, your name and your score head
   the Memories tab; under them the month, its albums and its photos run
   exactly as they do today. There is no Profile sheet any more.
2. **The score is the sum of your wins by size**: Quick 1, Regular 2, Deep 3.
   It is `BlockSize.massTier`, which already exists and already says 1, 2, 3.
3. **It is counted from the record every time, never stored**, so deleting a
   win lowers it and Undo puts it back with no extra code.
4. **It is yours alone in v1.** Not on a crew member's card, not ranked, not in
   a notification. The one question for the owner is whether crews ever see it.
5. **Press the score** for what it is made of (Deep, Regular, Quick) and the
   trend chart Profile has today. Press your head for Edit Profile (name,
   colour, look, earlier heads).
6. **Settings moves to a gear in the Memories header**, where Profile's button
   was.

### 2.1 How Snap Score is computed and perceived

- **Opaque by design.** Snap's own help page: a "super-secret, special
  equation" of Snaps sent and received, Stories posted and "a couple other
  factors". (b) [Snap help](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/7012326657044-What-is-a-Snapscore)
- **It can never go down.** The same page: there is "no way to lower or reset"
  it. (b)
- **It can be bought faster.** Snapchat+ has a Snapscore Multiplier, and a
  subscriber can see how much a friend's score changed since they last
  looked. (b) [Multiplier](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/25046593810964-How-does-Snapscore-Multiplier-work-with-Snapchat),
  [friend's change](https://help.snapchat.com/hc/en-us/articles/9483580485012-How-do-I-see-how-much-my-friend-s-Snapscore-has-changed)
- **Perceived as a popularity figure.** Parent guides describe teens reading a
  low score as low popularity and using the app more to raise it; the
  stronger, older reporting is about Snapstreaks "keeping score of
  friendships". (b, weak: guides and a 2016 Business Insider piece)
  [Gabb](https://gabb.com/blog/what-is-a-snap-score/),
  [BI 2016 copy](https://stoneham-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/419/Teen-Shapchat-Scores-PDF)
- **Why the opacity is valued, and why it is wrong here** (c): a secret
  formula cannot be gamed precisely and invites curiosity, which suits an
  engagement metric. Some Wins' score is a statement about your own record,
  and the owner's brief is itself a formula (1, 2, 3) and a rule (deleting
  lowers it). Transparent, reversible and private is the opposite of Snap on
  all three counts, on purpose.

### 2.2 What a score does to motivation

- **Points raise output, not motivation.** Points, levels and leaderboards
  increased the quantity of work but not intrinsic motivation, autonomy or
  competence; they acted as progress indicators. (a) Mekler et al.,
  *Computers in Human Behavior* 71, 2017,
  [link](https://bruehlmann.io/publication/mekler-towards-2017/) (already in
  `docs/research/game.md`)
- **Expected, contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation; informational
  feedback does not.** (a) Deci, Koestner and Ryan, *Psychological Bulletin*
  125(6), 1999,
  [PDF](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf).
  A score that only describes your record is feedback; one that unlocks or
  buys something is a reward. (c)
- **Comparison with peers cuts motivation in young people.** 84 pupils aged 13
  to 14 wore Fitbits for eight weeks; the in-app leaderboard and peer
  comparison reduced competence and autonomous motivation, and competition gave
  only short-term effects. (a) Kerner and Goodyear, *American Journal of Health
  Education*, 2017, via
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1522458). The app's launch users are
  college students and crews are friends, which is the same setting. (c)
- **Upward comparison is mostly negative** in fitness communities, softened by
  close ties. (a, abstract only) Poncin et al.,
  [UCLouvain](https://dial.uclouvain.be/pr/boreal/en/object/boreal%3A289963).
  Social comparison is a drive people do not need prompting for. (a) Festinger,
  "A theory of social comparison processes", *Human Relations* 7, 1954.
- **A measure that becomes a target stops measuring.** (a) Strathern's
  statement of Goodhart's law, *European Review* 5(3), 1997. Duolingo's XP
  farming is the shipping example. (b)
- **Losing visible progress hurts.** Broken streaks shown in a log reduce
  engagement (a) Silverman and Barasch, 2023 (cited in
  `docs/profile-and-head-plan.md` §2). A score that drops on delete is a
  visible loss, so the drop must read as the record being accurate, not as a
  penalty. (c)

## 4. The wins score

### 4.1 Definition

```
score = Σ over every HabitLog where completed == true and habit != nil
        of habit.blockSize.massTier          // Quick 1, Regular 2, Deep 3
```

| Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Completed logs only? | **Yes.** | A win is a completed log; it is what the tower, the albums and the Wins tally count. |
| Skipped? | **No.** | Not a win. Also free: no live code sets `skipped` (§1). |
| Planned, not done? | **No.** | A `PlanItem` is not a log until it is done; Your Three and plan outlines are intentions. |
| A friend's win you see in a crew? | **No.** | It is a `SharedWin` on your phone, not your log. |
| A win you logged from a crew's slot? | **Yes.** | It is a local log (`logCrewWin`). |
| Deleted? | **Goes down by its size, at once.** Undo puts it back. | Derived from the rows `WinDeletion` removes and restores. |
| Resized after the fact? | **Moves with it.** | `blockSize` is read live. |
| Remove Win from Crew (not delete)? | **No change.** | The local win stays. |
| Reset All Data | **0.** Restore from a backup recomputes it. | Derived. |
| Time span | **All time.** | It is identity, like Snapscore and Strava's all-time row. A month figure belongs on the month, not the header. (c) |

**Never stored.** No counter in `UserDefaults`, no SwiftData field, no
CloudKit column. A stored total drifts the first time a delete, an undo, a
restore or an iCloud merge misses it; a sum over the record cannot. (c)

**Cost.** One `FetchDescriptor<HabitLog>` with `#Predicate { $0.completed }`,
`relationshipKeyPathsForPrefetching = [\.habit]`, summing in memory, done in
the view model's existing `load` and on the same refresh signal the calendar
already uses. Do not predicate on the enum through the relationship; SwiftData
predicates on enums are unreliable (c). Ten thousand logs is a few
milliseconds; measure it with `PerfProbe` before calling it fine.

**Numerals.** `StrataFont.digits` gives "1204". At 12 points a day a heavy
user passes 1000 in about three months (c). The owner's call: keep the app's
ungrouped digits, or group with `.formatted()` for this figure only.

### 4.2 Where it shows

| Place | v1 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Memories header | **Yes**, the hero figure. | The brief. |
| Your Score sheet | **Yes**, with the breakdown. | Transparency. |
| A crew member's card (`CrewInfoSheet` people list) | **No**, until he decides. | §4.4. |
| Wins tab, goal crest, widget, replays, notifications | **No.** | One home. A score in a notification is scorekeeping (`docs/voice.md`: "Streaks celebrate the habit and never scorekeep against people"). |

### 4.3 Animation

- `.contentTransition(.numericText(value: Double(score)))` on the numeral. With
  `value:` the digits roll up when it rises and **down when a win is
  deleted**, so the drop is seen as the same number correcting itself. (b)
  Apple, [`numericText(value:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/contenttransition/numerictext(value:))
- Change it inside `withAnimation(GridConstants.motionSnappy)` when the
  record changes; no roll on first appearance (it arrives at its value).
- Reduce Motion: `GridConstants.crossFade` (the page's own pattern).
- No haptic, no sound, no confetti, no colour change on a drop. (c)
- Accessibility: one element, label "Score, 412", trait button, hint "Shows
  how it's made".

### 4.4 Should crews see it?

**Recommendation: not in v1.** If he wants it later, show a **crew score**
on the crew's own page (the sum of the wins sent to THAT crew, which every
phone in it can already count from `SharedWin`s), never a person's all-time
total, never sorted or ranked. (c)

Why: the comparison research (§2.2), the voice rule against scorekeeping
against people, the crew design that already refuses to name who has not
posted (`CrewStats.streakLine`), and the fact that your all-time score
includes wins you never sent to anyone. Showing it would publish a count of
private wins. (c) Sending it would also need a new field on `CrewMember`
(`Strata/Social/CrewModels.swift:73`), which today carries only a name, a
head, a join date and a photo, and so a change to what crews receive.

## 6. Risks and how to avoid them

| Risk | What it looks like | Avoid it by |
|---|---|---|
| **Gaming by many Quick wins** | Holding the slot three times equals one Deep. The weights do not stop it. (c) | Nothing to win by gaming: no audience, no rank, no unlock, no level, no "best score". Mekler's finding (§2.2) is that points raise output; without an audience the only output worth raising is your own honest record. (a, c) |
| **Inflation, the number stops meaning anything** | All-time figures only grow; 4,000 says little. | The header carries the all-time figure; Your Score shows this month's and the split by size, which is where meaning lives. Never add multipliers, bonuses or double-score days (Snapchat+, Duolingo). (b, c) |
| **A drop reads as punishment** | You delete a mistaken win and your number falls. | Roll down quietly, no colour, no line. The footer says it plainly once. Undo restores it in the same breath. (c) |
| **Undermining intrinsic motivation** | Logging becomes "for points". | It is informational feedback, not a reward: not spendable (Finch), not contingent on anything, unlocks nothing. Deci et al. (a) |
| **Comparison and shame in crews** | A friend sees 900 against your 120. | Private in v1; if ever shared, a crew-level figure, unranked (§4.4). Kerner and Goodyear (a). |
| **Clutter, the opposite of the brief** | A stats row, badges, a highlights row and a chart all above the calendar. | One number above the fold. Streak as one line. Chart and breakdown behind a press. Instagram itself has been moving rows off the top (b). |
| **Losing the month's lead** | Header pushes the calendar below the fold. | Header held to about 250pt; measure at 402x874 and on an SE-size screen before it ships. |
| **Two numbers disagree** | Score counts a log the calendar does not, or the reverse. | One predicate, shared: `completed && habit != nil`, the same the albums and replays use. A value test: delete, undo, resize, restore, reset each move the score exactly as §4.1 says. (c) |
| **Regressions from moving Profile** | Debug flags `-strataOpenSheet settings`, `-strataScrollProfile head`, screenshots of screen 12 and 19. | Re-point them at Memories' stack (Settings) and Edit Profile (head). |
| **The XP history** | `b189dae` removed XP; `game.md` warns against a second currency. | This is not a currency. Record the decision and its guardrails in `docs/design.md` so the next pass does not "fix" it in either direction. |
